# General Health and Wellness Clinical Reference Guide

## 1. Fever and Viral Illness Management
A fever is defined as a body temperature of 100.4 F (38 C) or higher.
Most mild fevers are the body's natural defense mechanism against viral or bacterial infections.
Home care recommendations for adults and older children:
- Rest and stay well hydrated by drinking plenty of water, clear broths, or oral rehydration solutions.
- Dress lightly and keep room temperatures comfortable.
- Over-the-counter antipyretics such as acetaminophen or ibuprofen can help relieve discomfort when taken according to package instructions.
- Red flags requiring urgent medical evaluation: persistent fever over 103 F (39.4 C), fever lasting more than 3 days, difficulty breathing, stiff neck, confusion, or severe chest/abdominal pain.

## 2. Common Cold vs. Influenza
The common cold typically presents with sneezing, nasal congestion, runny nose, sore throat, and mild cough. Symptoms generally develop gradually over 1 to 3 days.
Influenza (the flu) tends to have a sudden onset with higher fevers, severe muscle or body aches, chills, extreme fatigue, and dry cough.
Prevention: Frequent handwashing with soap and water, annual influenza vaccination, and avoiding close contact with individuals who are sick.
Treatment: Supportive care, adequate sleep, fluids, and saline nasal sprays.

## 3. Hydration and Dehydration Prevention
Adequate daily water intake is essential for organ function, joint lubrication, and temperature regulation.
Healthy adults generally need approximately 2.5 to 3.5 liters of fluid per day from food and beverages, adjusted for heat and exercise.
Signs of mild to moderate dehydration include dry mouth, dark yellow urine, headache, lightheadedness, and lethargy.
Severe dehydration requires immediate medical care and IV fluids.

## 4. Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)
Normal blood pressure is generally defined as systolic under 120 mm Hg and diastolic under 80 mm Hg.
Hypertension often has no noticeable symptoms and is known as a silent condition.
Lifestyle interventions: Reducing dietary sodium (aiming for less than 2,000 mg/day), engaging in at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic exercise weekly, maintaining a healthy weight, managing stress, and limiting alcohol intake.
Regular monitoring with an automated home blood pressure cuff is recommended for individuals diagnosed with hypertension.

## 5. Diabetes Management and Blood Sugar Control
Type 2 diabetes involves insulin resistance and elevated blood glucose levels.
Key management pillars:
- Nutrition: Focus on fiber-rich vegetables, lean proteins, whole grains, and minimal refined carbohydrates and added sugars.
- Physical Activity: Regular exercise improves insulin sensitivity.
- Monitoring: Regular HbA1c testing (typically aiming for less than 7% for most non-pregnant adults, as personalized by a doctor).
- Symptoms of hypoglycemia (low blood sugar): trembling, sweating, dizziness, confusion, rapid heartbeat. Treat with 15 grams of fast-acting carbs (e.g. 4 oz fruit juice).

## 6. Sleep Hygiene and Mental Well-being
Adults need 7 to 9 hours of quality sleep per night.
Tips for restorative sleep: Maintain a consistent sleep-wake schedule 7 days a week, keep the bedroom cool, dark, and quiet, avoid screens and blue light 60 minutes before bed, and restrict caffeine consumption in the afternoon and evening.
Chronic sleep deficiency is associated with cardiovascular strain, impaired immunity, and mood disorders.
